C5.26: Calculators that compute only from figures somebody published. An affordability estimate, a cost range, a rebate, a catch value — all arithmetic, and all things a visitor will reasonably read as a promise. So the constants are not stored on the calculator: it names them by attestation key, which means a rate used in a sum cannot be a number nobody stood behind, and the correction that fixes the published rate fixes every figure computed from it.

The calculation is a list of named steps with a closed vocabulary of seven operations and three kinds of operand — a literal the owner typed, an answer the visitor gave, or a published fact. It is never an expression string and never evaluated as code: an owner configuring arithmetic must not be a way to run code on the instance, and "we sanitise it first" is how most of the ways that goes wrong begin.

What it refuses is the feature. A constant nobody has published, or one past the date it was good for, stops the calculation and names the figure at fault — no default, no last value it saw, no term quietly dropped from the sum. A payment computed from a stale rate looks exactly like one computed from today's and the reader cannot tell, so the only honest thing a page can do with an out-of-date constant is decline the arithmetic. Division by zero is refused rather than rendered as Infinity. Every result carries the owner's assumptions — the column is NOT NULL, because an optional caveat is one nobody writes — and the as-of date of its oldest input, which is how old the answer really is. Publishing is refused while any constant it needs is unpublished, so a calculator cannot go live destined to fail its first visitor.

C6.18: Service areas that can actually be checked. `radius` and `regions` describe coverage to a reader; neither answers "do you come to L4C 2K1?" without a geocoder or a gazetteer. A third kind, `postal_codes`, is checkable exactly, and `locations.checkCoverage` answers one of three things: covered, outside, or unconfirmed. The third is the point — for a radius, a named region, or a business that has said nothing, the answer is never "covered", because the failure worth designing against is a van sent two towns over on the strength of software answering a question it had no way of answering, in the business's voice.

Both surfaces are plain GET forms, server-rendered, with no client component and no server action. Only the visitor's own numbers travel in the URL and the figure is recomputed on every render, so a crafted link cannot display a result the calculator never produced — and the answer is shareable, which a redirect-and-flash pattern would have thrown away. The builder at `/admin/calculators` never offers a box that takes a formula: every operand is chosen from a list — a number the owner types, an answer the visitor gives, a figure the business published, or an earlier step — which is the safety property made visible rather than merely documented.
