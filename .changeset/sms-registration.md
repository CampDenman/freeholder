Texting in the US and Canada needs the carriers' permission first, and this now
says so before you find out the hard way.

The hard way is worth explaining, because it is what makes this the commonest
way a text-message launch fails: an unregistered US number does not bounce. The
carrier takes the message, tells you it worked, charges you for it, and then
quietly drops it. Nothing anywhere says it never arrived.

So Freeholder works out what each of your numbers needs — 10DLC brand and
campaign registration for ordinary US numbers, verification for US and Canadian
toll-free ones, and whether a sender name is even permitted where you are
sending — and refuses to send from a number that has not got there yet. It
refuses rather than warns, because a warning you can click past leaves you in
exactly the position this is meant to prevent.

You record how far along each registration is as you go through your provider's
forms, including why one was rejected if it was. The screen tells you which
number is blocked and on what, and when nothing can send it says which of the
possible reasons is the real one — no numbers, all switched off, none healthy,
or one waiting on paperwork — instead of a single unhelpful message that sends
you to check the thing that is already right.
