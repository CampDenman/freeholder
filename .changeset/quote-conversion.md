Accepting a quote now starts the work. The moment a customer says yes,
Freeholder opens the project, issues the agreement if you asked for one, puts
the appointments you scheduled in the diary, and drafts the deposit and balance
invoices — all at once, or none of it, so you are never left with an invoice
and no job to explain it.

You decide what each quote sets in motion. A kitchen refit and a one-hour
consultation are not the same job, so the plan lives on the quote rather than
in your settings.

Invoices arrive as drafts. Freeholder never sends money requests on its own and
never guesses a tax treatment on your behalf — you review and issue them.
Appointments only appear for times you actually gave it: a quote carries a
price, not a date, and inventing one would put a fiction in your diary.

Everything lands on the same customer. Not a copy, not a new "billing contact"
— the person who accepted is the person on the invoice, the agreement and the
appointment.
