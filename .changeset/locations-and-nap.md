Your address, phone number and opening hours now live in one place, and
everywhere they appear on your site comes from that one place. Add them under
Locations in the admin, or during setup, and they show up in your footer, in
your site's structured data for search engines, and on a page of their own at
/locations. Change them once and every one of those follows.

That last part matters more than it sounds. Search engines decide whether to
trust a local business partly by checking whether the address on its website
matches the address in directories — and "Unit 3, 210 Fifth St" against
"210 5th Street #3" reads as two different businesses. Freeholder writes your
address exactly one way, everywhere, so it cannot drift apart over time.

Opening hours understand the difference between "closed on Sunday" and "we
haven't said anything about Sunday". Only the first is something a search
result can tell a customer, so state a closure rather than leaving the day
blank. Addresses are written the way each country writes them: a French
address puts the postcode before the town, a British one gives the postcode
its own line.

If you travel to your customers rather than the other way round, you can say
which areas you cover — either everywhere within a distance of a point, or a
list of named places — and keep your street address off the site entirely.

If you work online and have no address at all, skip it. Nothing appears on
your site, and no empty address block turns up in your footer.
