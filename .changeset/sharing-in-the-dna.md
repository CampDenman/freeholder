Every public page on your site can now be shared, and you can see what came of
it. A share bar appears at the bottom of each page — copy the link, use your
phone's own share sheet, or send it straight to WhatsApp, email, a text
message, Facebook, X, LinkedIn, Reddit or Telegram. It works without
JavaScript, so it works for everybody.

Each share creates its own short link, like `/s/k3mq7bt2xp`. Following one
lands on the ordinary page, and the visit shows up in your traffic figures as
having come from sharing — which means the sharing report and the traffic
report are the same numbers, not two counts that disagree. The Sharing screen
in your admin lists every link, who sent it if you know them, how many people
came, and how many of those got in touch.

You can switch sharing off for anything you would rather not have circulating —
one photograph gallery, one page, one product. Switching it off does not just
hide the buttons: the links people have already sent stop working too. That is
the whole point of the setting, and it is the part most tools quietly get
wrong.

Channel controls distinguish “all” from “none,” so clearing every channel does
not accidentally turn every sharing option back on. Removing an unpublished
target also asks you to confirm that its existing tracked links will stop.

You can also give a page its own headline, description and image for social,
separate from the ones search engines see. A search result is read by somebody
already looking for you; a shared link is read by somebody who was not. Leave
them blank and the page's own title is used, with a branded card generated
automatically.
