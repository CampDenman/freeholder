Some appointments need something done before they are really on. A service can
now ask for an intake form and a signed waiver, and Freeholder will take the
booking, hold the slot, and simply decline to confirm it until both are done —
because a customer who cannot book until they have signed something is a
customer who leaves. You can always confirm it anyway; somebody who signed on
paper in front of you has met the requirement.

Waivers are properly signed, not ticked. The customer reads the actual words,
types their own name, and Freeholder records who signed, when, from where and
with what, along with a fingerprint of the exact text — so editing your waiver
next month changes nothing about what somebody agreed to last month. Signing
happens once and the link stops working afterwards.

Reminders arrive on their own. Confirming an appointment schedules them — the
day before and two hours before by default, and you can set your own — and
every one is recorded: sent, or not sent and exactly why. Moving an appointment
moves its reminders; cancelling one stops them. Text reminders are held back
until Freeholder owns the messaging side properly, and say so rather than
failing quietly.

Customers do all of this from the link in their email. No account, no password,
and nothing to ask you for.
