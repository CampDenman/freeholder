You can follow catalogues of ready-made playbooks now, and install from them.

A catalogue is just a web address you decided to trust. There is no central
store to be approved by, and Freeholder follows none until you add one.

Before you install anything you see what it would be allowed to do, the
instructions it would follow in full, who wrote it, and whether it works with
the version you are running. Approving is approving those exact contents — if
the catalogue changes the entry after you have read it, installing is refused
and you are asked to look again.

What arrives is instructions and nothing else. A definition that tries to bring
along an API key, a connected account or a named worker is rejected outright
rather than quietly cleaned up. What you install arrives switched off and
pointed at nobody, for you to hand to a worker of your own.

Freeholder remembers where everything came from — the catalogue, version and
exact contents — even if you stop following that catalogue later.
