You can now send one message to a whole audience. Under Newsletters →
Campaigns, pick wording you have already written and a group you have already
defined, give it a name, and send it — now or at a time you choose.

Sending starts the campaign rather than finishing it. Copies go out in batches
over the following minutes, so a campaign to ten thousand people cannot fall
over halfway and leave you guessing; you can pause it at any point and carry on
later, and it picks up exactly where it stopped rather than starting again.

The audience is frozen the moment a campaign starts. Someone who joins the
group afterwards is not on that send, someone who leaves still is, and months
later you can still say exactly who it went to.

One bad address no longer stops the rest. If someone has unsubscribed or an
address has bounced before, that copy is recorded as not sent — with the
reason — and everybody else still receives theirs. Bounces and spam complaints
reported afterwards are recorded against the campaign that caused them rather
than against whichever campaign mailed that person most recently.

Campaigns only go to people who agreed to hear from you. Confirming a
newsletter subscription now records that agreement against the contact, and
unsubscribing records that it was withdrawn — so a campaign built on any
audience still skips anybody who has said no, and says so on the campaign page.

Every number on the campaign page is counted from what was actually recorded
for each person, not read from your mail provider's dashboard. If your provider
loses a message, a figure stops rising rather than quietly becoming wrong.

There is a "send test" button so you can read the thing yourself before
committing to the list.
