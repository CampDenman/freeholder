Traffic now has a second page: visit to paid. It shows how many people reached
each step of your business in a period — visited, became a contact, asked for
something, agreed to buy, paid — and how many were refunded, kept deliberately
apart from the steps rather than drawn as one of them.

It counts people rather than clicks. Somebody who read nine pages is one
visitor, and somebody who was a stranger last week and gave you their name
today is one person in both steps, not two.

What is in each step depends on what you use. Quotes appear if you send
quotes, baskets if you sell things, bookings if you take appointments — and
nothing appears for a part of the platform you have switched off.

Underneath the chart is a plain-English line for every number, saying exactly
what it counts: whether a draft quote is included, whether a cancelled booking
still counts, whether a payment is dated when the invoice was written or when
the money arrived. There is the same for the two ways of crediting a campaign,
first touch and last touch, which frequently disagree.

These are counts for the period rather than one group of people followed
forward, so somebody can turn up at a later step without appearing at an
earlier one — the customer who walked in or was recommended. Rather than hide
that, each step also says how many of its people were in the step before it.
