Projects gather a job into one place. Start one for a customer — or for
yourself, because your own work is still work — and attach the quote they
accepted, the agreement they signed, the appointments in the diary, the hire
that went out and the invoice that is still unpaid.

Nothing is copied. Everything on a project is a link to the real thing, so the
invoice shown here is the invoice in your ledger and there is never a second
answer to what somebody owes.

Each project keeps a list of what still has to happen, so the work inside the
work sits beside the paperwork rather than in another tab. You can record what
the job achieved too — "time on site up 40%" — with a field for how you
measured it, and Freeholder points out when that field is empty, because a
claim you cannot back up is one you should not publish.

Photographs pair properly: a before and an after belong together rather than
being two uploads that happen to be next to each other.

This is the same record your public case studies will be built from later, so
finishing a job and showing it off are the same thing rather than two.
