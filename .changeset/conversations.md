One conversation with each person, whatever it arrived on. A contact form
submission, the email reply to it, and a text about the same job now sit in one
thread on that person's record — not three separate places to check.

Every message shows which door it came through, so a mixed thread stays
readable. Replying picks the channel of whatever they last used, because
somebody who emailed you and then texted expects the answer by text.

A text from a number you have never seen becomes a real contact with a real
history, rather than an orphan message with a phone number attached.

For anything sent, you see what actually happened to it rather than what was
hoped: queued, sent, delivered, read — or the carrier's own reason it failed,
word for word, which is what you need when you ring them about it. What each
message cost is recorded too, because texting is the one thing here where money
can leave by accident.
