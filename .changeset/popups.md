Popups, announcement bars and exit-intent messages, built the same way you
build a page.

A popup is written in the block editor you already use, so it can hold a
heading, a picture, a button — anything a page can. Choose its shape (a dialog
over the page, a bar along the bottom, a small card in the corner) and when it
appears: straight away, after a few seconds, partway down the page, or when the
cursor leaves the window.

Say where it appears — one page, a section of the site, or everywhere — and who
sees it, using the same saved groups your campaigns and price lists already
use. "Everyone except people already on my list" is one of the choices, so you
stop asking subscribers to subscribe.

Then say how often. Two a week means two a week: the count is kept per visitor
and survives them closing the tab, and closing a popup is treated as an answer
that stands for as long as you set. Nobody gets the same interruption twice a
day because they came back on Tuesday.

If it collects an email address, you have to write the sentence that goes
beside the tick box — the platform will not save a sign-up popup without it —
and those exact words are kept as the record of what that person agreed to.
Names a newsletter and they get the usual confirmation email; leaves it empty
and the tick box is the consent. Either way the address becomes a contact like
any other, not a list off to one side.

Every popup can be closed with the keyboard. The dialog is a real dialog, so
Escape closes it, focus stays inside it while it is open and goes back where it
was afterwards, and the close button is the first thing a screen reader
announces. A popup that would break any of that cannot be switched on.

The screen shows how each one has done: shown, closed, and addresses collected
over the last thirty days.
