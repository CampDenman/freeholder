Reports. A new page showing what the business earned, who it earned it from,
and — under every figure — exactly how that figure was arrived at.

Revenue is money that actually arrived: an invoice counts in the month it was
paid rather than the month it was written, and anything refunded comes back
out. Each currency is reported on its own. Nothing is ever converted to make
one bigger-looking total, because the invoice you send and the money you
receive are in the same currency and a report should not pretend otherwise.

You can cut that revenue by service, by product, or by location. What each cut
covers is written on the page, including where it stops: revenue by location
covers bookings and not shop orders, because an order records where it was
sent rather than where it was rung up, and guessing one from the other would
give you a number that looks right and is not.

Revenue by product is counted from item prices, and the page says so, because
that total will not match your revenue figure — discounts, postage and tax
belong to the whole invoice rather than to any one item, and splitting them
across items by proportion would invent a decision you never made.

There is a cohort report — what the customers who first paid in a given month
went on to spend since — and the visit-to-paid funnel, which asks the traffic
pages rather than defining a second version of the same thing.

Views can be saved by name. A saved view keeps the question rather than the
answer, so opening one always shows today's figures, and it is checked when
you save it rather than months later when you open it.
