You can now translate your pages from the admin. If your site publishes more
than one language, Translations appears in the menu and shows every page
against every language you offer, so the gaps are in one place. Until now the
database could hold a translation and there was no way to write one except
through the API.

Translating a page shows your words on the left and a box for each one on the
right — the heading, the paragraphs, every question and answer in an FAQ, in
the order they appear on the page. You are never shown a block, an image path
or a brace: the list is worked out from the page itself, so a new kind of
block becomes translatable the moment it holds words.

Two things follow from doing it this way, and both are deliberate. The
structure of the translated page is the structure of the original — you cannot
end up with a French page that has an extra section nobody remembers adding.
And anything you leave blank is shown in your own language, so a half-finished
translation is a readable page rather than a page with holes in it.

A translation is only shown to visitors once you tick "checked by a person".
Until then the page is served in your own language and the translation stays a
draft, which is what stops a machine-drafted translation going live without
anybody reading it.
