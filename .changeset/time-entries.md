Time tracking, so you bill what you worked rather than what you remember.

Start a clock on a job, or record hours after the fact — "90", "1:30" and
"1.5h" all work. Stopping rounds up to whatever increment you bill in, because
a business that bills in fifteens is saying a twenty-minute call costs half an
hour.

Set an hourly rate for the business, for a person, or for one particular
project; the most specific one wins. Whatever rate applied when you recorded an
entry stays on that entry forever, so putting your prices up next month never
re-prices work you have already done.

When it is time to bill, tick the hours and Freeholder turns them into a draft
invoice in one step — one line per entry, with what you wrote and how long it
took, rather than a single "consulting" line nobody can check. Every hour you
bill is stamped with the invoice it went on, so it can never be billed twice,
and an invoiced entry can no longer be quietly edited.

Unbillable work is worth recording too. It sits in your history at nothing,
which is how you find out where the time actually goes.
