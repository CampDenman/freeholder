C8.14: Guided assessments an owner authors end to end. The owner writes the questions, the answer options and the scores each option carries, then writes the outcome bands a total can land in — and a respondent is only ever shown a band the owner wrote. Nothing composes outcome text, so an assessment cannot diagnose, certify, guarantee or recommend, which is what makes a triage questionnaire safe to put on a clinic, trade or lender site. A single answer can escalate past the score to an authored instruction ("leave the building and call the gas emergency line"), shown above the band rather than instead of it.

Two rules are enforced by the database rather than by the service. `assessment_bands_no_overlap` is an exclusion constraint, so two bands can never cover one score and the outcome can never depend on row order; `assessment_responses.band_id` is `NOT NULL` and restricts deletion, so an unauthored outcome is unrepresentable and a band somebody was already shown cannot be deleted out from under them. Publishing is refused until the bands cover every score the questions can reach, so a gap is a refusal the owner sees while editing rather than a stranger meeting mid-enquiry.

The public surface is a server-rendered block with no client component: the redirect carries the band's key and the words are read from the owner's row, keeping attacker-supplied text out of the outcome. Responses join the contact spine through `contacts.resolve`, repoint on merge, and export and erase under the new `assessments.responses` privacy scope. Also adds MASTER.md §4.18 and principle 13 — owner-authored truth — which C8.15, C8.16, C5.26 and C6.18 build on.

The admin is at `/admin/assessments`. The question builder puts the score beside each answer rather than behind a settings panel, because the owner is authoring the judgement and not just the wording, and it shows the range those questions can reach while they are being written. The outcome editor shows coverage live against the same `coverageGaps` the service refuses with, so "no outcome covers 4 to 7" appears next to the outcomes rather than arriving as a surprise at publish time, and deleting an outcome somebody has already been shown is refused with the count of people who saw it. Escalations pick their trigger from the questions that exist, so a rule that could never fire cannot be written, and the answer queue marks escalated responses rather than leaving a reader to interpret a number.

A repeated post resolves to the answer already stored rather than producing a second: a page issues a nonce, the response carries it, and a partial unique index makes the duplicate impossible. The concurrent case goes through `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` rather than catching the violation, because a constraint error aborts the transaction and the re-read would fail too — so somebody who merely double-clicked would have seen an error for doing nothing wrong. Being told a second time that your boiler is urgent is not a duplicate row; it is the same judgement arriving twice as though it were two.

Ships with a demo heating check (English, French and Spanish) that loads, verifies and purges through the tracked demo run, chosen because it shows the two things prose cannot: an answer that outranks the score, and an outcome nobody composed. A real-browser spec proves the whole journey — an owner refused a gap, fixing it, publishing, a visitor answering, an escalation outranking a low score, and the owner seeing it marked — with axe WCAG A/AA clean on every new surface in both light and dark.
