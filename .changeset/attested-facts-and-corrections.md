C8.15: Published figures now carry where they came from and when they were true, and correcting one never erases what it replaced. `core/attestations` is a single table behind a rate, a metric, an opening status and a byline, because they are the same thing — something the business asserts, that somebody stood behind, at a moment that can be named. Storing them one way is what makes "no unattested claim" a rule the codebase can keep rather than a promise each module re-makes in its own way.

Nothing is ever edited. A correction is a new row that supersedes the old one, and the old one keeps its value, its source and its dates — so the correction ledger the two publishing editions asked for is the storage model rather than a feature beside it, and a rate history exists without anybody building one. Two guarantees sit in the database rather than the service: `attestations_current_idx` allows exactly one current fact per key and subject, so "what is the rate today" can never have two answers that differ by row order, and `attestations_one_correction_idx` allows a fact to be superseded once, so the history cannot fork. The first is written by hand because a business-wide figure has no subject, and NULLs do not collide in a plain unique index — every such fact would otherwise have been exempt from the one rule that matters most.

The service refuses more than it accepts, deliberately. Asking for a fact nobody stated returns null: no fallback, no last known value, no neighbouring subject, because a surface with nothing to say should say nothing. A correction dated earlier than the value it replaces is refused as a typo rather than accepted into a ledger that would then read backwards. A fact past its `validUntil` is returned and marked stale rather than hidden, since "we last knew this on Tuesday" is honest and silence about a number the business published is not. Withdrawing unpublishes everywhere without deleting the row.

Owners work at `/admin/facts`, sorted by as-of rather than by key so the oldest thing still on the public site is the first thing they see. There is no edit button anywhere in it. The ledger page shows what the figure is now above everything it has been, each with its date, its source and why it changed. A `fact` block renders one on a page with its date and source together — never the number alone — and can show the corrections publicly, because a correction only the owner can see is not a correction.
