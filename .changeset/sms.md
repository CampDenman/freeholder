Text messages, sent and received. Connect a Twilio account and the numbers you
already hold appear here — what each one can do, which country it belongs to,
and whether it can actually send right now.

That last one matters more than it sounds. The commonest way a text-message
launch fails is a number that looks perfectly fine in every console and is being
quietly filtered by the carriers. So the health check shows three answers, not
two: it can send, it cannot send and here is why, or nobody could ask. A green
tick that nobody verified is exactly the failure it would be hiding.

Numbers are read in from your provider, never bought here. Buying one spends
your money on their terms, in a country with its own rules about who may hold
it, and that belongs in their console where you can see what you are agreeing
to.

Texts people send you arrive in the same inbox as their emails and form
submissions, on the same thread, and a text from a number you have never seen
becomes a real contact rather than an orphan message. Replies now go out by text
where the conversation is a text conversation.

Every message records what it cost and how many parts the carrier billed —
texting is the one thing here where money can leave by accident — and what the
carrier actually said happened to it, in the carrier's own words.
