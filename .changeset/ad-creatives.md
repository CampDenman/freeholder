Your ad positions now actually show ads.

Upload the artwork, give it a click-through and a description, and it runs in
the positions you sold it into — at the size those positions declare, on a
laptop and on a phone, from one placement. A text-only sponsor line is there
too, for a newsletter or a blog where a banner would look wrong.

Space nobody has bought falls back to your own promotion, if you asked it to.
Your own promotions need nobody's approval but yours; anything somebody paid
for has to be looked at before it appears, and goes back for another look the
moment its artwork or its destination is edited.

Every ad is labelled as one, on the page, and there is no setting that turns
that off.

Visitors leave through your own link rather than the advertiser's, so a click
is counted and the destination cannot quietly be swapped for a different one
after you approved it. A link that has been tampered with goes nowhere.

And when it is time to bill, a campaign raises an ordinary draft invoice
against the advertiser's contact — the same invoice, tax and reporting path as
anything else you sell. You issue it from the invoice, where the tax question
is asked properly.
